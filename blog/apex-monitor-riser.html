Apex: printed parts meet a wooden shelf

Workshop42 project notesLocal review draft

TypeMonitor riser
PartsFour printed legs + wood board
DesignTriangular supports
NotesLocal review draft

A monitor riser that fits your desk

Apex is a set of four 3D-printed legs for a wooden monitor shelf. It raises the screen and leaves room underneath for a keyboard and other desk items, while letting you choose a board that suits your space.

The idea is to print the parts that make the shelf work and use a board for the surface. You can choose the size and finish of the board, then print the legs in a color that fits your setup.

Why the legs look like that

The triangular legs give the riser its geometric shape and hold the board above the desk. Countersunk screw holes let the legs attach underneath with a clean finish. The board is the part you can adapt to your desk; the printed legs bring the pieces together.

Planning your build

Choose a board to suit your desk. The MakerWorld notes describe material between one and two inches thick, four printed legs and four M3 or M4 wood screws, with screw length chosen for the board. Place the legs near the corners, mark the holes and pre-drill where the wood is thick or prone to splitting, then screw the legs on from underneath.

Printing the legs

My listed PLA settings were 0.16 mm layers, five walls and 5% infill, with no supports or brim. The listing also suggests PETG for heavier monitors. My own example uses a one-inch board and a 36-inch ultrawide monitor; that experience is not a general load rating. Check the model and profile before printing and choose a board and fastening suitable for your setup.
